Five Habit Findings to Put Into Practice

Research Oct 5, 2026

Small facts can start a useful conversation. Here are five findings selected from the research material behind Quests, expanded with sources and context so you can decide how they relate to your own routine.

Each practical idea below is our application of the research. The studies examined particular interventions and populations. Use them to inform your plan and notice what works in your own life.

1. Decide what you will do when a cue appears

The finding: A review of 94 tests found that if-then planning improved goal attainment on average.

Try it: finish the sentence, If I finish breakfast, then I will... Choose a concrete action and a place to do it.

Keep in mind: goals and study settings varied. An if-then plan needs a cue you can recognize and a response you can carry out.

2. Record progress you can review

The finding: A review of 138 randomized studies found that progress monitoring improved goal attainment. Recorded and publicly reported outcomes were linked with stronger effects across the studies.

Try it: make a brief check-in after your action, then review the pattern with someone you trust.

Keep in mind: study averages leave room for different personal experiences.

3. Give a routine a familiar context

The finding: Ebert and Lin studied 127 working, midlife adults who were insufficiently active. Consistent walking contexts helped automaticity develop and persist at follow-up. Walking levels themselves were not better maintained in that group.

Try it: link your action to a familiar part of your day.

Keep in mind: this trial studied walking. A stronger feeling of habit and sustained activity are separate outcomes.

4. Support can be part of the setup

The finding: Wing and Jeffery’s weight-loss study reported 95% completion among friend recruits with added support, compared with 76% among solo recruits in standard treatment.

Try it: agree with a friend on what encouragement will be welcome.

Keep in mind: those groups differed in both recruitment and treatment. These percentages describe that specific program.

5. A fresh start can help you begin

The finding: Three archival studies observed increased diet searches, gym visits, and goal commitments after landmarks such as new weeks and birthdays.

Try it: choose a start date that means something to your group.

Keep in mind: the research measured initiation. Maintaining a routine requires continued action.

Turn one idea into a shared plan

Choose one action, a cue, a way to check in, and a date to review it. The challenge planner puts those decisions on one page. You can print a copy, save the text, or bring the agreement into Quests.

Sources

Research checked October 5, 2026. Practical suggestions are our interpretation of these findings.

  1. Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006), implementation intentions meta-analysis
  2. Harkin et al. (2016), progress monitoring meta-analysis
  3. Ebert and Lin (2024), consistent contexts and walking habits
  4. Wing and Jeffery (1999), friend recruitment and social support in a weight-loss program
  5. Dai, Milkman, and Riis (2014), temporal landmarks and goal initiation
