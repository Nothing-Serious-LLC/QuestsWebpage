Choose Different Personal Goals for a Shared Challenge

Planning and templates Nov 16, 2026 · By Quests

Friends can share a challenge even when they want different things from it. Agree on a shared theme and review date, then let each person choose a specific action and schedule. Write down how updates work before starting. This guide includes a decision worksheet for a group that cannot agree on one activity.

Use this for your own group agreement. An organized event or app may require a particular daily action. Check those requirements before treating a personal alternative as eligible for completion or rewards.

Start with what each person wants

Ask everyone to finish three sentences privately or in the group: “I want more time for…”, “I could realistically do…” and “I would like support with…”. Each person can share a practical preference without explaining a health condition, work situation or personal reason.

Collect the answers before proposing a challenge. A group that wants time together may prefer a weekly shared session. A group whose members want different kinds of practice may prefer separate actions and one shared reflection. These are planning options you can test in a short round.

Choose between three formats

  • One activity, personal amounts. Everyone reads, for example, with their own chosen format, duration and dates. Agree on whether audiobooks or reading aloud fit your group's purpose.
  • One theme, different actions. “Make time for creativity” could include drawing, writing and playing music. Each person defines their own practice in advance.
  • Separate goals, shared support. People keep individual plans and meet for a weekly review. The common commitment is the support conversation.

For a fitness or wellness theme, each participant chooses an action appropriate to their abilities and circumstances. A friend can help organize a schedule; medical suitability belongs with qualified guidance. The healthy challenge ideas guide offers adaptable starting points.

A worked example: three friends with different goals

Asha wants to draw, Ben wants to read more and Cam wants to practise guitar. A daily drawing challenge would leave two people pursuing an activity they did not choose. They agree on a two-week “Make time for practice” round with personal actions and a Sunday review.

AshaSpend 10 minutes sketching one objectTuesday and Thursday, four sessions total“Sketch session done”; artwork optional
BenRead for 15 minutes in a chosen formatMonday, Wednesday and Friday, six sessions total“Reading session done”; book title optional
CamPractise a chosen guitar passage for 10 minutesSaturday and Sunday, four sessions total“Practice done”; audio optional

On Sunday, each person shares one thing that helped and one adjustment for the coming week. They use a separate record for each action. Minutes, pages and musical passages stay in their original units. Their manual agreement recognizes showing up for the planned action, with no overall winner.

If Ben completes five of six sessions and Asha completes three of four, record those counts beside their actual plans. The fractions describe adherence to different commitments. They cannot establish who worked harder or gained more. Use the completion-rate calculator when you need help handling planned opportunities and unknown records.

Resolve a disagreement before the start

Suppose Cam wants a leaderboard and Asha wants an informal practice group. Ask what each person hopes the leaderboard would provide. If Cam wants a visible record, a personal session count may fit. If Cam wants a competition and others do not, offer separate optional rounds. Agreement on participation comes before choosing the scoring system.

Use this opening: “We want different things from the format. Would a shared Sunday review with separate activity plans work for this round?” Give people room to decline. The fair-rules guide explains scoring choices once the group has agreed what it wants to do.

Review the fit after one round

Ask whether the action was clear, the schedule was realistic and the updates felt useful. Keep a record of the original plan and the date of any revision. Choose the next round together using the weekly review worksheet.
