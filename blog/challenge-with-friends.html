How to Start a Challenge With Friends

Guide Updated Oct 8, 2026

Quests starts with a simple idea: self-improvement can be a shared journey. Pick something you care about, give it a place in your day, and invite people who will encourage you along the way.

A useful group challenge has a clear action, a check-in everyone understands, and a date to reflect together. Use this guide to agree on those details, then make a printable plan with the free challenge planner.

Choose one action you can describe clearly

Write the rule so a friend can tell what you mean. Try ten minutes of reading, five minutes of sketching, or a short walk that fits your day. If your group has different needs, choose a shared theme and let each person set a suitable target.

For example, a reading group could agree to spend time with a book each day. One person chooses ten minutes; another chooses five pages. Decide together whether listening to an audiobook counts. Our challenge ideas can help you choose a starting point.

Give the action a cue

Connect your action to a moment you recognize: after breakfast, when you close your laptop, or when you sit down on the train. Then write an if-then plan: If I finish breakfast, then I will read for ten minutes at the kitchen table.

A meta-analysis of 94 tests found that specific cue-and-action plans improved goal attainment on average. The effects varied across settings. This is a reason to make your plan concrete.

Set a first round and a review date

Choose seven days to try the arrangement, fourteen to practice it for longer, or thirty when the group wants a longer round. These are planning options. The end of a challenge is a chance to review what fits your life; forming an automatic habit can take longer.

Choose a shared start date. Research on fresh starts observed increased goal initiation around landmarks such as a new week or birthday. Long-term follow-through needs its own plan.

Invite people with a specific proposal

Start with people who are interested in the action and comfortable sharing progress. Choose a group size that lets you respond to each other. A pair can keep coordination simple; larger groups can agree on smaller check-in circles.

Want to try seven days of reading together? I’m planning ten minutes after breakfast. We can each choose our own target, check in that evening, and compare notes next Sunday. If a day gets busy, we’ll adjust and keep going.

Use the accountability partner guide if you want help choosing someone and agreeing on boundaries.

Agree on the check-in and the support

  • Where: choose one place for progress, such as a shared note or a Quest.
  • When: agree on a time that works across everyone’s schedule and time zone.
  • What: a short update about the action and anything you want to share.
  • How to help: ask whether people want reminders, encouragement, or practical help.

Keep sharing comfortable. A photo, personal reflection, or explanation for an absence should be each person’s choice.

Decide what happens on a difficult day

Write a fallback before you need it. That might be two minutes of reading when ten will not fit, or returning at your next planned opportunity after a missed day. If you use a smaller action, agree how to record it honestly.

Keep the original target visible, and revise future targets together when they stop fitting. Any plan you write here is your group’s agreement. Check the app for how it counts check-ins, points, and streaks.

Make the journey worth sharing

Notice specific effort: a friend making time on a busy day, finding a book they love, or coming back after a break. Pick a small shared celebration for the review date, such as a conversation about what you learned.

At the end, ask what felt manageable, what got in the way, and whether you want another round. Keep the useful parts and change the rest.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good first challenge with friends?

Choose an action everyone can adapt to their own day, such as reading, drawing, or a short walk. Agree on what counts and use a short first round to learn what works for your group.

How many people should join?

Choose a group size that lets people respond to each other. A pair keeps coordination simple. Larger groups can use smaller check-in circles. There is no universal ideal group size.

Will a 30-day challenge make a habit automatic?

Habit formation varies by action, person, and context. Treat thirty days as a period to practice and review your routine, with the option to continue afterward.

Does the planner create a Quest in the app?

The planner creates a plan you can copy, download, or print. You create or choose the challenge separately in Quests.
