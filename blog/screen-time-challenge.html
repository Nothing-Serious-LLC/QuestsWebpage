The 30-Day Screen Time Challenge: Less Phone, With Friends

Challenges Sep 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Your phone already tracks the number. Open Screen Time on iPhone or Digital Wellbeing on Android and you will see a daily average, probably between three and five hours. The 30-day screen time challenge is a rule about that number, checked in on every night, with friends who can see whether you hit it.

It is a strange challenge because the phone you are trying to use less is also the scoreboard. That turns out to be fine. The number is honest, and honesty is the point.

Set the limit from your baseline

Do not pick a number off the internet. Check your seven-day average, then:

  • Baseline minus 30% for the first 30 days. Four hours becomes 2 hours 50 minutes. Cut deeper later.
  • Or a hard cap of two hours if your baseline is already under three.

Everyone in the group uses the same formula and their own number. The check-in is binary: under your limit today, yes or no, per the phone's own report at midnight.

Decide what counts

Screen time includes maps, calls, music and the camera, which are not what anyone is trying to cut. Two ways to handle this:

  1. Total screen time, simple. Everything counts. Easy to verify, slightly unfair to people who navigate a lot.
  2. Social and entertainment only. Both platforms break the total down by category. Set the limit on Social plus Entertainment plus Games. Fairer, slightly more work to check.

Pick one on day one. Most groups choose the category version.

Let the settings do most of the work

Willpower is a poor plan. Change the phone:

  • App limits. Set a daily limit on the three apps that eat the most time. When it hits, the app locks. Yes, you can override it. Make yourself do that consciously.
  • Grayscale. Turning the display to grayscale removes most of the pull from feeds. It is uncomfortable for two days and then normal.
  • Notifications off for everything except people. No app needs to interrupt you.
  • Home screen cleared. Nothing on the first page except tools. Feeds live in a folder on page three, where you have to type the name to find them.
  • Charge it outside the bedroom. The first and last 30 minutes of the day are where most screen time hides. Buy a cheap alarm clock.

The four weeks

Week 1: Measure and remove

Make the settings changes above and hit your limit most days. Notice when you reach for the phone: queues, elevators, the pause between tasks. Just notice.

Week 2: Replace

Every reach has a trigger, usually boredom or discomfort. Have a replacement for the common ones: a book in the bag, a podcast for the walk, a paper list for the task pause. The reading challenge and this one pair naturally.

Week 3: The relapse week

Something stressful happens and the phone is the comfort. Expect it. The group check-ins matter most this week. A miss is a dent; check in tomorrow.

Week 4: Lower the limit or hold

If you have hit the limit 20 or more days, consider cutting another 30 minutes for the last week. Otherwise hold. The goal of week 4 is a week you could repeat forever.

Running it with friends

This challenge has an awkward feature: the check-in needs the phone. Keep it to one tap. A shared Quest in Quests with the rule "under my limit today," checked in from the lock screen widget or in ten seconds before bed, is about as little phone as an accountability tool can ask for.

Group rules that help: screenshots of the weekly report on Sundays, shared with the group, so the numbers stay honest; and a group agreement about what to do with the time. One group we know set a rule that every reclaimed hour on a weekend had to become one message to a friend they had not spoken to in a month. The general guide to running a challenge with friends covers group size and what to do when someone drops.

What you get back

Cutting 90 minutes a day is over ten hours a week. That is a book a week, a fitness habit, a language, or simply more sleep. Decide what the time is for before you start; unassigned time drifts back to the phone. Some people find ten minutes of meditation a useful first thing to put in the gap, because it trains the same muscle: noticing the reach and choosing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good screen time goal per day?

Start from your own baseline and cut 30% for the first 30 days. If you are already under three hours, a two-hour cap is a reasonable target. Numbers taken from other people's phones rarely stick.

How do you do a screen time challenge with friends?

Everyone uses the same formula (baseline minus 30%) and their own number, checks in each night on whether they stayed under it, and shares the weekly screen time report on Sundays. A shared roadmap where everyone's check-ins are visible does most of the work.

Does grayscale really reduce screen time?

For many people, yes. Removing colour takes most of the pull out of feeds and games. It feels uncomfortable for a day or two and then becomes normal.

What should I do with the time I used to spend on my phone?

Decide before the challenge starts. A book in the bag, a podcast for walks, an alarm clock in the bedroom. Unassigned time drifts back to the phone; assigned time does not.
