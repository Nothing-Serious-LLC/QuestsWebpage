Building Habits as a Shared Journey

Research Updated Oct 5, 2026

Quests exists to help people make self-improvement a shared journey. A habit becomes something you can practice with friends, talk about honestly, and celebrate together.

That idea has three practical parts: people who support you, a plan you can repeat, and visible progress that feels rewarding. Research can inform those choices while keeping the limits of each study in view.

Social: agree on support that feels useful

Choose people who want to take part. Explain the action you are working on and ask what encouragement would help them. A shared reading goal, creative practice, or everyday walk can give you something to check in about.

In a study of 166 adults in a weight-loss program, completion reached 95% for participants recruited with friends and given added social-support strategies, versus 76% for solo recruits receiving standard treatment. Both recruitment and treatment differed. The result is specific to that program.

For your group, start by agreeing how you will respond to progress and missed days. Personal reflections and photos can remain optional. Our partner guide includes an invitation and an agreement you can adapt.

Structured: turn the intention into an action

Write down what you will do, the cue that will remind you, and the place where it will happen. An example is reading for ten minutes at the kitchen table after breakfast.

Gollwitzer and Sheeran’s meta-analysis found that if-then plans improved goal attainment across varied settings. Your practical next step is to connect a recognizable moment with a specific action.

Choose a first round that fits your schedule and set a review date. At that review, ask whether the target and cue worked. Give yourself room to revise the plan.

Visible: record enough to learn from the journey

A check-in can record what you did, what helped, and what you want to try next. Decide with your friends how much detail to share.

A meta-analysis of progress monitoring found benefits for goal attainment, with stronger effects when outcomes were recorded or shared. These are findings across interventions; the study did not evaluate Quests.

In the app, check-ins, milestone paths, points, streaks, and encouragement give progress a visible place. Different groups enjoy different features. Agree on whether competition feels motivating and what your group wants to celebrate.

What this means for a first challenge

  1. Choose an action each person can adapt to their own needs.
  2. Connect it to a cue that already happens in the day.
  3. Agree on check-ins and welcome forms of encouragement.
  4. Write a fallback for a difficult day.
  5. Choose when to review the experiment together.

Frequently asked questions

What is social accountability for habits?

It is an agreement to share progress with other people and offer useful support. The group decides the action, check-in rhythm, and boundaries together.

Has research proven that Quests improves habit formation?

The studies discussed here evaluated specific behavioral interventions and populations. They inform practical ideas for shared habits. They did not evaluate the Quests app.

Can I build habits on my own?

You can use a clear cue, a manageable action, and a record of progress on your own. Invite others when their involvement feels useful and welcome.
