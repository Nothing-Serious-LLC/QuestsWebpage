The Complete Guide to Dry January

Challenges Sep 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Dry January started as a public health campaign in the UK in 2013 and is now one of the most popular 30-day challenges in the world. The idea is simple: no alcohol for the month of January. Finishing it is less simple. This guide covers what to expect and how to make it to February 1.

Why people do it

Common reasons include better sleep, more energy, saving money, losing a few pounds, and a reset of the habit of reaching for a drink by default. Many people also report that the biggest benefit is noticing when they wanted a drink and why, which changes how they drink for the rest of the year.

If you drink heavily every day, talk to a doctor before stopping suddenly. Dry January is designed for moderate drinkers who want a reset.

What to expect, week by week

Week one: the habit loop shows itself

The first few evenings can feel oddly empty at the time you would normally pour a drink. This is the cue firing with nothing to do. Have a replacement ready before the cue hits.

Week two: sleep improves

Alcohol fragments sleep, especially the second half of the night. Most people notice deeper sleep and clearer mornings somewhere in the second week.

Week three: the social test

By now you have probably faced a dinner, a party, or a Friday. The "just one" pressure peaks here. See the section on social pressure below.

Week four: the finish and the decision

Energy is usually up, spending is down, and the month feels doable. The useful question for the last week is what you want February to look like.

Handling social pressure

  • Order first. Get a non-alcoholic drink in your hand before anyone asks.
  • Have a one-line answer. "I'm doing Dry January with a few friends" ends the conversation. Explaining or apologizing invites negotiation.
  • Bring someone. One other non-drinker at the table changes the entire dynamic.
  • Leave early if you want to. You do not owe anyone the last hour.

What to drink instead

Alcohol-free beer, wine, and spirits have improved enormously. Sparkling water with bitters, a good tonic with lime, ginger beer, kombucha, or a properly made mocktail all give you something to hold and sip. The point is a drink you actually enjoy in the slot where alcohol used to go.

Why doing it with friends works

The single strongest predictor of finishing a challenge like this is having other people doing it with you. In one well-known study, 95% of people who joined a health program with friends completed it, against 76% who went alone. Dry January is easier to start alone and much easier to finish together.

If you slip

One drink on January 14 does not undo thirteen dry days. Mark the day honestly and check in again on the 15th. The research on habit formation is clear that a single miss barely registers; two in a row is where things unravel.

After January

Many people carry the habits forward: alcohol-free weekdays, a drink cap on weekends, or a second dry month later in the year. Whatever you choose, decide it with the group that got you here. See how to run a 30-day challenge with friends for the next round.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do Dry January if I start late?

Yes. Thirty-one consecutive days is the challenge; the calendar month is just the tradition. Start when you are ready and count 31 days.

What if I have one drink during Dry January?

Log it honestly and continue the next day. One drink does not undo the benefits of the dry days around it. The goal is the month, not perfection.

Is Dry January safe for everyone?

It is designed for moderate drinkers. If you drink heavily every day, stopping suddenly can be dangerous, so speak with a doctor first.
