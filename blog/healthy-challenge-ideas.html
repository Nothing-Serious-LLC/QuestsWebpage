50 Healthy Challenge Ideas to Do With Friends

Challenges Updated Nov 16, 2026 · 9 min read

A healthy challenge with friends starts with one practical action, a personal target and a review date. Choose from the 50 ideas below, adapt the rule to each person's circumstances and agree on what an update looks like. A seven-day trial gives you a bounded plan to review together.

These are activity ideas and planning examples. Choose exercise, food and sleep changes that fit your abilities, needs and any professional advice you follow. Participation, photos and personal health information can stay optional.

Choose a challenge your group can actually use

  • Pick one action. Write a rule you can recognize when it happens, such as preparing tomorrow's lunch.
  • Choose the dates. Start with a seven-day trial, or select a longer round with a clear review point. A challenge's end date provides a chance to adjust; habit development varies.
  • Keep personal targets personal. People can choose different amounts or alternatives. Use the different-goals worksheet when the group wants a shared theme.
  • Agree on updates. “Done”, “missed” and “not recorded yet” are enough for a simple manual record. An unknown update stays open until clarified.

NIDDK's habit-change guidance recommends planning around barriers, recording progress and reviewing setbacks. The examples here are original group-planning suggestions; results will depend on the people and the action.

Movement and activity challenges

Choose a manageable duration or amount for each person, and include rest or alternative days where needed.

  1. Personal step plan. Rule: follow your agreed step target on selected days. The step challenge guide includes a worked group plan.
  2. Short walk. Rule: take a walk of your chosen length on agreed days, using a route that fits your needs.
  3. Movement break. Rule: take a comfortable movement break at one planned point in your day.
  4. Existing strength routine. Rule: follow the strength session already appropriate for you and record completion.
  5. Dance break. Rule: move to a song you enjoy, with seated or standing options.
  6. Movement preparation. Rule: set out the clothes or equipment you need for tomorrow's planned activity.
  7. Accessible route check. Rule: choose a suitable route before the week's shared outing.
  8. Scheduled run. Rule: follow your existing suitable running plan, including its rest days.
  9. Beginner activity plan. Rule: choose a plan appropriate to your starting point and check in on its session days.
  10. Mobility practice. Rule: complete a comfortable mobility routine you already know is suitable for you.
  11. Active errand. Rule: choose a safe, feasible errand to walk, wheel or cycle during the week.
  12. Try a new activity. Rule: explore one appropriate activity during the round and share whether you want to repeat it.
  13. Activity appointment. Rule: reserve a realistic time for your chosen movement and review how it fitted your day.

Food and drink planning challenges

Choose ideas that fit allergies, dietary needs, budget and access to food. A group can organize preparation without setting identical nutritional targets.

  1. Drinks ready. Rule: prepare access to your usual suitable drink for a planned outing or work session.
  2. Read one label. Rule: look at a food label and note one thing you learned about a product you use. For a related food-focused format, see the no-added-sugar challenge guide.
  3. Alcohol-free social plan. Rule: arrange one gathering around an activity and suitable alcohol-free options. If alcohol dependence or withdrawal may apply, get medical support before reducing or stopping. NHS guidance explains the withdrawal risk.
  4. Produce preparation. Rule: prepare a fruit or vegetable you enjoy for a meal or snack.
  5. Cook together. Rule: choose one recipe the group can adapt to dietary needs and make it during the week.
  6. Tomorrow's meal plan. Rule: decide what you will eat at one busy point tomorrow and prepare what you need.
  7. Breakfast preparation. Rule: set up a breakfast option that suits your routine.
  8. Caffeine observation. Rule: privately note when you have caffeine and whether the pattern is worth discussing or changing.
  9. Plant-based recipe. Rule: try one suitable plant-based dish and share any useful preparation tips.
  10. Packed meal. Rule: prepare a meal to take with you on one agreed day.
  11. Learn a cooking skill. Rule: practise one safe preparation skill, such as chopping a familiar vegetable.

Rest and evening-routine challenges

Pick an action you can choose, such as preparing a wind-down routine. The time you spend in bed and the sleep you actually get are separate observations.

  1. Wind-down cue. Rule: start a short, chosen wind-down activity at a time that fits your schedule.
  2. Sleep opportunity plan. Rule: review tomorrow's obligations and reserve a realistic opportunity for sleep.
  3. Phone charging spot. Rule: place the phone in your chosen charging location, keeping necessary alarms and access available.
  4. Morning preparation. Rule: prepare one thing tonight that will make tomorrow morning easier.
  5. Screen stopping point. Rule: choose when to finish one optional screen activity in the evening.
  6. Outdoor pause. Rule: spend a manageable period outdoors when conditions and access allow.
  7. Rest check-in. Rule: note whether tomorrow's plan needs a lighter option or a rest day.

Mind and learning challenges

  1. Meditation practice. Rule: choose a comfortable practice length and record whether you tried it. See the meditation challenge guide.
  2. Gratitude note. Rule: write one thing you appreciated, keeping it private if you prefer.
  3. Journal entry. Rule: write a short entry using a prompt from the journaling guide.
  4. Reading session. Rule: read or listen in your chosen format for an agreed period. Use the reading challenge guide to plan together.
  5. News window. Rule: choose a deliberate time to check the news while preserving access to essential information.
  6. Quiet pause. Rule: take a short pause with an activity you find comfortable, such as looking out of a window.
  7. Language practice. Rule: practise a small set of words or a short conversation.
  8. One task at a time. Rule: give one chosen task a short period of focused attention.
  9. Observation photo. Rule: photograph one interesting detail, respecting other people's privacy.

Screen and attention challenges

Keep work, care, accessibility and emergency uses available. Choose one optional pattern to review.

  1. Screen-use baseline. Rule: look at your phone's usage report and choose one category to observe. The screen-time guide offers a planning framework.
  2. Phone-light meal. Rule: put optional browsing aside during one agreed meal.
  3. First chosen action. Rule: do one planned morning action before opening an optional app.
  4. Notification review. Rule: review one app's notifications and keep the ones you need.
  5. Intentional app session. Rule: decide what you came to an app to do, then close it after that task.

Social and connection challenges

  1. Arrange a call. Rule: invite someone to a mutually convenient conversation.
  2. Specific appreciation. Rule: tell someone you know about one thing you appreciated, when welcome.
  3. Shared meal. Rule: plan one meal together in person or by video, with flexible participation.
  4. Thoughtful message. Rule: send a welcome note or reply to someone you have been meaning to contact.
  5. Talk and move. Rule: arrange a conversation alongside an activity that suits everyone, including seated options.

A seven-day group example

Three friends choose the theme “Make space for a break”. Jo plans a short walk on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Lee chooses a comfortable seated movement break on those days. Sam chooses a quiet outdoor pause on Tuesday and Thursday. Each person confirms their own action before the round starts.

The group records five agreed activity dates across its calendar and eight personal opportunities: three for Jo, three for Lee and two for Sam. On Sunday, everyone shares what fitted their routine and what to adjust. Keep the individual plans beside any totals. Different actions and schedules have different demands.

Copy this start message: “Would you like a seven-day break challenge? Choose an action and dates that work for you. We will use a brief update after each planned activity and review together on Sunday. Photos and explanations are optional. You can pause or revise your plan.” The invitation templates offer other formats.

Set up the round

Use the challenge planner to write down the dates and action, then agree on fair group rules. For coworkers, use the workplace guide to keep participation voluntary.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest healthy challenge to start with?

Choose an action that fits your current routine and needs, such as preparing a meal, taking a manageable movement break or reading for a short period. Agree on your own target and trial dates.

How long should a healthy challenge last?

Choose a bounded trial, such as seven days, and review what worked. A 14-day or 30-day round is another planning option. Habit development varies, so a challenge end date cannot guarantee automaticity.

How many people should be in a group challenge?

Choose a size that allows everyone to agree on the plan and receive the support they want. A pair can start together; a larger group can use smaller voluntary check-in groups. There is no single best number for every format.

Can we do a healthy challenge remotely?

Yes. Agree on personal actions, local activity dates and an update location. Use a shared review time that everyone can attend, or leave asynchronous updates.
