How to Run a Reading Challenge With Friends

Challenges Sep 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Most reading challenges are a number of books per year. Fifty-two, one a week. They fail for the same reason most yearly goals fail: the feedback is too slow. Miss two weeks in February and the whole thing is behind, and behind feels like over.

A daily challenge fixes that. The rule is a small number of pages, the check-in is tonight, and the books take care of themselves. Twenty pages a day is about 7,000 pages a year, which is 25 to 30 average books without ever thinking about the count.

The rule

Read 20 pages a day for 30 days. Paper or e-reader. Any book, fiction or nonfiction. Rereads count.

Agree on the edges before day one:

  • Audiobooks: 20 minutes equals 20 pages is the usual rule. Some groups exclude them. Decide once.
  • Articles, news and social feeds: out. The point is sustained attention on one thing.
  • Comics and graphic novels: in, at 40 pages.
  • Work reading: out, unless you would read it anyway.

Why 20 pages

Twenty pages takes most adults 20 to 30 minutes. That is short enough to do on a hard day and long enough to get somewhere in a book. It is also a floor. On a good day you will read 60 and check in the same way.

The alternative rule, "read for 20 minutes," works too and is easier to measure with audiobooks. Pages are more satisfying because you can see them go by.

Reading is the habit most people have lost

The habit is quietly disappearing. A 2021 Pew Research survey found that 23% of American adults had not read a book in any format in the past year. American Time Use Survey data shows average daily leisure reading has fallen for years and now sits well under 20 minutes a day, against roughly three hours of television. None of this is a moral failure. It is what happens when the alternatives are engineered to be easier. A shared daily rule is a way to make reading the easy option again for 20 minutes.

Make it a group without making it a book club

Book clubs fail because everyone has to read the same book at the same speed. Skip that. In this format, everyone reads whatever they like and checks in daily. The group part is light:

  • Day 1: each person posts what they are starting.
  • Every day: one check-in. In Quests that is one tap on the shared roadmap.
  • Sundays: one sentence each. What you read, whether it is good. Ten minutes, total, in a chat or a call.
  • Day 30: everyone recommends one book from the month to the group.

The Sunday sentence is what makes it fun. People end up borrowing each other's books, which means the challenge feeds itself.

Where the 20 minutes come from

  • Replace the last scroll. Phone charges outside the bedroom; book on the pillow. This one change is enough for most people.
  • Carry the book. Waiting rooms, trains, the ten minutes before a meeting. Paper or a reader app with the current book on the home screen.
  • Pair it with coffee. Twenty pages with the first coffee is a strong morning cue.
  • Quit bad books fast. A rule of 50 pages then stop is what keeps the challenge alive. Nobody keeps a daily habit with a book they dread.

When someone falls behind

A miss is a dent. Agree that on day one. Two misses in a row and someone in the group asks what they are reading, which is a better prompt than "you missed a day." If the book is the problem, change the book. See how to keep a streak when life gets busy for the general version.

After day 30

Keep the rule. Twenty pages a day is a permanent habit for many people once the first month is done, because the books you are in the middle of pull you forward. Some groups run a second Quest with a shared theme (one classic, one book from a country you have never been to, one recommended by another member) to keep the Sunday sentences interesting. If your group wants a different kind of quiet habit next, the journaling challenge pairs well with reading.

Frequently asked questions

How many pages a day should I read to build a habit?

Twenty pages is a good daily floor: 20 to 30 minutes for most adults, small enough to do on a hard day and enough to finish 25 to 30 books a year without tracking a book count.

Do audiobooks count in a reading challenge?

That is your group's call. The common rule is 20 minutes of audiobook equals 20 pages. Decide before day one so nobody argues about it later.

How do you do a reading challenge with friends without reading the same book?

Everyone reads their own book and checks in daily on the shared page target. Add one sentence each on Sundays about what you read. The daily check-in gives the accountability; the Sunday sentence gives the conversation.

What if I hate the book I'm reading?

Stop at page 50 and pick another. A daily reading habit only survives with books you want to open. Quitting a bad book is part of the challenge.
