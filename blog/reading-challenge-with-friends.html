How to Run a Reading Challenge With Friends

Challenges Updated Oct 12, 2026 · By Quests

Run a reading challenge with friends by choosing a manageable reading target, agreeing on which formats count and sharing brief progress updates. Everyone can choose their own book and personal target while keeping the same start and review dates.

The plan below is an adaptable example. Try a short first round, then decide what you want to continue. Choose the schedule around the time you actually have available.

Find a reading partner or a small group

Start with a friend who has mentioned a book, someone in an existing book club or a reading group you already participate in. A library discussion group or adult class may offer a shared context; ask the organizer whether a challenge invitation fits their rules.

Ask for a small first commitment: “Would you like to read for ten minutes on three evenings this week and compare notes on Sunday?” You can both choose your own book. Use the invitation templates to include the dates and a clear reply question.

For a new acquaintance, begin with the amount of contact and personal information you are comfortable sharing. The accountability partner guide helps you discuss expectations.

Choose a target each person can use

A target could be five pages, ten minutes or one short chapter. Reading speed and page length vary, so ask each person to test a target against their own book and schedule. Keep the minimum clear enough to recognize when it is complete.

  • Print and ebooks: choose time or pages and record the same unit consistently.
  • Audiobooks: agree on listening time if the group includes them. Listening minutes can stand as their own measure.
  • Comics and graphic novels: choose a time target or a personal page target that suits the format.
  • Articles or work reading: include them when they fit the group's stated purpose.

For example, one person could read a novel for ten minutes while another listens to an audiobook for ten minutes. Each records whether they completed their chosen action. There is no need to invent a pages-to-audio conversion.

A worked first week

In this illustrative plan, Maya reads five pages after breakfast and Dev listens for ten minutes on the journey home. They choose their own books and plan sessions on Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

  • Before Monday: agree on the books, personal targets and where updates belong.
  • After each planned session: post a brief completion update. Add an optional spoiler-free sentence about the reading.
  • On Sunday: compare what fitted their week and decide whether to repeat or change the plan.

Maya completes three sessions. Dev completes two and misses Friday. They keep those records accurate and discuss whether Friday needs a different cue. The selected-days worksheet can hold this schedule.

Make the conversation worth having

Choose a light prompt: What made you want to keep reading? Which idea stayed with you? Would you recommend this book to someone with similar interests? Agree on spoilers before sharing plot details.

When people read different books, they can still share what they enjoy about reading. When everyone wants the same book, agree on a discussion section and date that suits the group. Use the check-in prompts for short updates between conversations.

Adjust an awkward plan

If the book has lost your interest, choose whether to change it, finish a section or pause. If the time is difficult, try another cue or a smaller future target. Discuss a missed session using the reminder preferences you agreed on.

Keep the distinction between the activity and its record clear. Someone can read and forget to update the group. An absent record stays unknown until that person clarifies it.

Choose what happens after the round

Use the finish reflection to decide whether to continue, change the target or finish the commitment. A challenge's end date is a useful review point. The habit-duration guide explains the limits of fixed habit timelines.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find accountability partners for a reading challenge?

Ask a friend who wants to read more or someone in an existing book club, library discussion group or class. Follow the community rules and propose a short first round with clear expectations.

How many pages a day should we read?

Choose a manageable personal target after trying it with your own book. Page length and reading speed vary. A time target is another option when formats differ.

Do audiobooks count in a reading challenge?

Include audiobooks when the group agrees. Record listening time as its own measure and define each person's target before the round starts.

Can everyone read a different book?

Yes. Share a check-in rhythm and review date while choosing your own books. Agree on spoiler boundaries and use short prompts to share what you enjoy.
