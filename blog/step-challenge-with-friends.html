How to Run a Step Challenge With Friends

Challenges Updated Oct 19, 2026 · By Quests

Start a step challenge by agreeing on a duration, an appropriate personal target, one counting source per person and a check-in routine. Decide how your group will recognize participation before comparing totals.

A phone or wearable may provide a step count when its tracking is enabled and the device is carried or worn. Check that each participant can access a usable record before the round begins.

Choose targets that fit the people joining

Let each person choose a target that suits their circumstances, abilities and any health guidance they follow. Review their usual activity and available time before setting a number. Keep participation voluntary and make room for a suitable alternative when step counting is inaccessible.

A shared daily number can be easy to explain, while personal targets can accommodate different routines. Discuss which format feels fair to your group. Avoid treating a popular step count as an appropriate prescription for everyone.

Agree on what you will compare

  • Personal completion: each person records whether they met their own agreed target.
  • Team participation: the group counts completed planned days across its members.
  • Individual totals: compare recorded steps only if everyone understands differences in work, time and device measurement.

For a manual completion example, four completed planned days out of five is 80%. The challenge scoring guide explains the tradeoffs. These group formats do not change points or streak rules inside an app.

Write the recording rules

  1. Choose one phone or wearable record per person.
  2. Agree on the day boundary and local time-zone handling.
  3. Decide where to post a completion update.
  4. Make screenshots and route details optional.
  5. State how the group handles a missing or corrected record.
  6. Allow a pause when participation stops fitting.

Devices can measure differently, and carrying a phone inconsistently can change its record. A small friendly group can acknowledge those limits and focus on the agreed participation goal.

Make a first-week plan

List realistic opportunities in your own schedule. A short walk during an available break or a familiar accessible route might fit. Account for weather, daylight, the environment and personal safety when choosing where and when to participate.

Use the free planner to record the action, cue and backup plan. Write an invitation that states the start date and asks friends whether the arrangement works for them.

Review the group experience

After the first week, ask whether targets were workable, recording was straightforward and reminders were welcome. Adjust the next round based on that discussion. For colleagues, the workplace challenge guide covers voluntary participation and privacy.
