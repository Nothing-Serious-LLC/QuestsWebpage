How to Run a Step Challenge With Friends

Challenges Sep 17, 2026 · 7 min read

A step challenge is the easiest group challenge to start. Every phone already counts steps, the rule is a number, and walking is something almost everyone can do more of. It is also easy to get wrong: a target set too high loses the people who need it most, and a leaderboard with no rules turns into an argument about who has the more generous phone.

Here is how to set one up so it lasts.

Pick the daily target

The 10,000 figure came from a 1960s Japanese pedometer marketing campaign, and the research since then tells a more useful story. A 2020 study in JAMA that followed almost 5,000 US adults found that going from 4,000 to 8,000 steps a day was associated with roughly half the risk of death from any cause, and the benefit kept growing, more slowly, up to 12,000. A 2022 meta-analysis in The Lancet Public Health found the benefit plateaued around 6,000 to 8,000 steps for adults over 60 and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults.

For a group with mixed fitness, that suggests:

  • 7,000 steps as the shared floor. Achievable with a 30-minute walk on top of a normal day.
  • 10,000 steps as a personal stretch target for people who want one.
  • A personal baseline plus 2,000 as an alternative rule for groups with very different starting points. Everyone checks their average from the past two weeks and adds 2,000.

The check-in stays binary: did you hit your number today, yes or no.

Write fair rules

  1. The phone's own count is the count. Watch counts are fine if the person always wears it. Agree on one source per person and stick to it.
  2. Midnight cutoff, local time. Whatever the phone shows at midnight is the day.
  3. Screenshots on request only. Trust by default. If the group uses a leaderboard, anyone can ask for a screenshot once a week.
  4. Illness and injury pause the streak. Say it before day one so nobody limps through a sprained ankle to keep a number.

Choose a format

Everyone hits the floor

The simplest and the one we recommend for a first challenge. The shared rule is 7,000 steps, every person checks in daily, and the group wins together if everyone finishes 30 days. No leaderboard needed.

Team total

Split into two teams of equal size. Each day, the team with the higher combined count takes the day. Best of 30. This works well for offices and larger friend groups because a strong walker lifts a weaker teammate.

Personal best

Everyone's score is the number of days they beat their own baseline. The person with the least active job can win. Good for groups where a raw leaderboard would demoralise half the members.

Keep it visible

Step challenges die in week two when the daily numbers stop getting posted. The fix is a dedicated place where everyone's check-in is visible without scrolling. Quests was built for this: one Quest, one shared roadmap, one tap to say you hit your number. Health data can auto-complete the check-in on days you cross the target, so nobody has to remember. If you use a chat instead, pin a daily message and update it.

Find the steps

Most people who miss a step target miss it by 1,500 to 2,500 steps, which is a 15 to 25 minute walk. Reliable places to find it:

  • Every phone call is a walk. Stand up when it rings.
  • Park at the far end. Get off one stop early.
  • A 10-minute walk after each meal. Three of them is about 3,000 steps and helps with post-meal blood sugar as a bonus.
  • Walk to the coffee shop and skip the delivery.
  • The evening "top-up" lap: check the count after dinner, walk the difference.

The 30-day arc

Week 1: hit the floor, learn where your steps come from. Week 2: the novelty fades and the weather turns; this is when the group's check-ins matter most. Week 3: add a group walk, in person or as a shared "walk and call." Week 4: hold the floor and decide together whether the next round moves the target up.

Frequently asked questions

How many steps a day should a step challenge require?

Seven thousand is a strong shared floor. Research links the largest health gains to going from about 4,000 to 8,000 steps a day, with smaller gains up to 10,000 or 12,000. Ten thousand works as a personal stretch target.

Is 10,000 steps a day necessary?

No. The 10,000 figure began as a marketing number. Studies show meaningful benefits at 7,000 to 8,000 steps for most adults and a plateau around 6,000 to 8,000 for people over 60.

How do you make a step challenge fair when people have different jobs?

Use a personal baseline rule: each person takes their average from the past two weeks and adds 2,000. Or use team totals so active walkers lift less active teammates.

What app is best for a step challenge with friends?
