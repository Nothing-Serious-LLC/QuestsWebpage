New Year's Resolutions With Friends: Why the Ones You Share Survive February

Guide Sep 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Resolutions have a bad reputation and a decent success rate, as long as you make them the right way. A large 2020 study in Sweden followed over a thousand people through a year of resolutions and found 55% considered themselves successful at twelve months. The people who did best had approach goals ("start running"); avoidance goals ("stop eating junk") did worse, and the study's authors noted that support made a measurable difference.

The version of a resolution that fails is the one made alone, phrased as an outcome, and never checked on. The version that works is a daily action, shared with a few people, checked in on every day. Here is how to set January up that way.

Turn the resolution into a daily action

"Get fit" cannot be checked in on. "Move for 20 minutes" can. "Read more" cannot. "20 pages a day" can. Every resolution on your list needs to become one sentence with a daily yes or no. If it cannot, it is a wish, and wishes belong on a different list.

Common translations:

  • Get healthier → 7,000 steps a day, or 20 minutes of movement.
  • Drink less → no alcohol in January (the guide), then a rule for February.
  • Less phone → under two hours of screen time (the plan).
  • Be more present → ten minutes of meditation (the challenge).
  • Save money → no-spend weekdays.
  • Learn something → 15 minutes of practice.

Pick one

People who make five resolutions keep zero. One habit for January, checked in on 31 times, is a result. In February you pick the next one, on top of the first. By spring you have three real habits. The research on habit formation is clear that a habit needs about two months of repetition to feel automatic, so stacking one a month is already aggressive.

Share it with two to five people

This is the step almost everyone skips. In Gail Matthews' goal study, participants who wrote down their goal, told a friend and sent weekly progress updates succeeded far more often than participants who kept the goal to themselves. In Wing and Jeffery's weight-loss study, 95% of people who joined with friends finished the program against 76% who joined alone. A resolution with witnesses is a different kind of promise.

The friends do not need the same resolution. They need the same rhythm: daily check-in, same start date, same finish date. A group of four with four different January habits works fine. The guide to running a challenge with friends covers group size and rules.

Use the fresh start, then stop relying on it

January 1 works because of what researchers call the fresh start effect: people are more likely to begin goals at temporal landmarks. It is a real boost, and it fades. Plan for the fade: the slump arrives around January 10 to 20, when the landmark is behind you and the habit is still effortful. That is when a friend's check-in on the shared roadmap matters most. It is also why a monthly restart, on the first, gives you twelve fresh starts a year.

A January plan for a group

Last week of December

Each person writes one resolution as a daily action. Share them in a group message. Agree on rules: check in before midnight, a miss is a dent, two misses in a row and someone checks in on you.

January 1 to 7

Start. Set up the check-in somewhere everyone can see. In Quests, that is one Quest per habit with everyone invited, or one shared Quest with a rule like "did my resolution today." Check in daily. Do nothing else.

January 8 to 21

The slump. Book one shared thing in this window: a dinner, a walk, a video call. Whoever is doing well that week sends one message to whoever is not.

January 22 to 31

Hold. Talk about February: same habit at a higher target, or the same habit plus one new one.

February 1

Fresh start number two. Most groups that finish January keep going.

If you have already broken it

It is not January anymore and the resolution is gone. Good news: Monday is a fresh start too, and so is the first of next month. Rewrite it as a daily action, tell one person, and start then. The date was never the important part. The daily check-in was, and that works in any month. For the science of why restarting fast matters more than never breaking, see the psychology of streaks.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of New Year's resolutions succeed?

A 2020 study of over a thousand people found 55% considered themselves successful after a year, with approach-oriented goals (start doing something) outperforming avoidance goals (stop doing something). Support and daily check-ins improve the odds further.

How do you keep a New Year's resolution with friends?

Each person turns their resolution into one daily action, everyone shares it, and the group checks in daily in one visible place. Friends do not need the same resolution, only the same rhythm and start date.

Should I make one resolution or several?

One. Check in on it every day for a month, then add the next one in February. Stacking one habit a month produces more lasting change than a list of five made on January 1.

Is it too late to start a resolution in the middle of the year?

No. Mondays and the first of any month give the same fresh start effect. Rewrite the goal as a daily action, tell one person, and begin at the next landmark.
